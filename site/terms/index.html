2. What LinkDish does, and what it does not do

LinkDish saves recipes from links and turns them into a portable recipe library. You can save from recipe websites, YouTube videos and Shorts, Pinterest pins, TikTok captions, pasted text, and photos of cookbook pages, recipe cards or screenshots.

From there you get a photo-first cookbook with search, favourites, tags, collections, ratings and a cook log; servings scaling and unit conversion (including oven temperatures); cook mode with timers; a weekly meal planner that builds one aisle-sorted shopping list; full backup and export; import from Paprika, Mela and JSON-LD; and, on Family, a shared household of up to 6 people.

What it does not do:

  • We don't host, publish, own or claim the recipes you save. They belong to whoever wrote them. We store a copy for you and keep the original source one tap away. We don't sell recipes, and we don't license them to anyone.
  • Extraction is best effort. LinkDish reads a page, video, pin, caption or photo and does its best to work out the ingredients and steps. Any given site can change its layout, block automated access, move behind a login, or simply break. Sometimes overnight, and without any warning to us. Some sources will stop working and stay broken for a while, or for good.
  • Results can be wrong. An amount, a temperature, a cooking time, a serving count or a whole ingredient can come out missing or misread. Ingredients and steps are worth a quick read before you cook from them.
  • Do not rely on LinkDish for allergen or dietary safety. Automated extraction can miss or misread an ingredient, and a recipe can change after you saved it. If you or someone you cook for has an allergy, an intolerance or a medical diet, check the original source and the product packaging every time. LinkDish is not a source of medical, nutritional or food-safety advice.

You don't need an account to try LinkDish. You do need one to buy or restore a plan, and to take part in Family household sharing.

You sign in with an email login code. Google sign-in is also offered, handled through Clerk, and Apple sign-in may be added later. Whatever you use, the account is the same LinkDish account.

A few things we ask of you:

  • One account per person. Don't share a login, or create accounts to get around plan limits.
  • Keep your details accurate, and keep access to your email secure. Anyone who can read that inbox can sign in as you, so treat it accordingly.
  • You're responsible for what happens under your account. Tell us promptly at support@linkdish.ca if you think someone else has access.
  • You can delete your account whenever you like, under Account in the app. Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription bought through Google Play or through our web checkout. Cancel that separately (see section 4).
  • If you own a Family household and delete your account, the household carries on. It passes to the member who has been in it longest. Only your own shared recipes, your shopping list items and the invites you sent are removed, and everything the others added stays. If the new owner doesn't have a Family plan, each remaining member gets a copy of the shared recipes in their own cookbook, as long as their cookbook has room. If you were the last member, the household closes.
