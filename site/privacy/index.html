LinkDish helps you turn recipe links into a clean, readable recipe format. This page explains what information the app handles and how it is used. LinkDish is available in Canada, the United States, the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom and elsewhere, and this policy applies wherever you use it.

Information LinkDish handles

  • Recipe links that you paste into the app so the service can extract recipe information.
  • Recipe photos or camera captures you choose to submit for image-based recipe scanning.
  • Pictures you choose for your saved recipes. We store a resized copy with its metadata (such as location) removed, at a private link that anyone holding the link can open, so your devices and household can show it. We delete them when you delete your account.
  • Recipe content returned from the extraction service, such as titles, ingredients, steps, and timing.
  • Recipes, edits, and personal notes you choose to save. Free includes up to 15 personal saved recipes; paid plans include unlimited personal saves.
  • Recipes, edits, and personal notes you choose to add to a shared Family recipe book, including the account email shown as the recipe owner.
  • Text and details from the page, post, caption or video you link to (such as the recipe text, title, author, site name and picture address), needed to extract the recipe.
  • An app-generated RevenueCat app user ID used to load purchase options, restore purchases, and verify LinkDish paid plan entitlement status.
  • Purchase, subscription, and lifetime-access status returned by RevenueCat, Apple App Store, Google Play, or RevenueCat Web Billing, such as whether a LinkDish Plus, Family, or Founding entitlement is active.
  • If you sign in for plan access or Family household sharing, your account email, login code/session records or social sign-in identity details, household membership, and household invite records.
  • If you choose to set them, a display name of up to 40 characters and an avatar emoji, shown to you and to the other members of your Family household.
  • If you join the iOS notification list, the email address you submit and basic request details needed to prevent abuse.
  • If you contact support, the email address and details you send us, such as the problem type, any link, and your device or browser.
  • First-party analytics events such as page or screen views, app opens, feature actions, extraction outcomes, source hostnames, app version, session identifiers, and sanitized error summaries.
  • In the Android app, crash and error reports sent to Sentry when the app crashes or stops responding: the error and where in the app code it happened, recent app events, the app version, and the device model and operating system version. LinkDish does not attach your account details to these reports, leaves out console logs, and removes query strings from web addresses in them.
  • On the linkdish.ca website only, page-view statistics collected by Plausible Analytics: the page visited, the referring site, browser, operating system, device type, and country. Plausible does not set cookies and, according to its data policy, does not store IP addresses.

How the information is used

  • Submitted links are sent to LinkDish's backend service, which fetches the page for you (identifying itself as LinkDishBot and following the site's robots.txt) and reads the recipe with LinkDish's own rules. For social and video links that LinkDish reads (TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and Pinterest; which of these are available can change), it reads the public caption, description or creator's comments, or, for a Pinterest pin, the recipe page the pin links to.
  • Ordinary recipe links and pasted text, and captions and descriptions that already hold a written recipe, are read by those rules only; their text is not sent to an AI service. Google's Gemini API, LinkDish's extraction provider, is used only for: photo scans (below), including tidying up garbled text in a recipe read from a scan; reading the recipe from the video itself when a TikTok, Facebook or YouTube video's caption or description has no written recipe, where video reading is switched on (the video's title, creator name and caption go with it; a TikTok or Facebook video file is downloaded, sent to Gemini and then deleted, and a YouTube video is passed to Gemini by its link); an "inspired" recipe you ask for from a YouTube video; and Fill in with AI (below). This is used only to provide recipe extraction, not for advertising. LinkDish uses the paid tier of Google's Gemini API in every country where LinkDish is available, including the EEA and the UK.
  • In the LinkDish iPhone and Android apps, LinkDish asks whether you allow Gemini processing before you first import a recipe or use Fill in with AI, and nothing is sent for either until you allow it. Your answer is remembered on that device, and you can change it at any time under AI data sharing in the app's Account screen. On the LinkDish website (app.linkdish.ca) there is no prompt first: using import or Fill in with AI on the website sends the content as described on this page.
  • When you choose Fill in with AI on a recipe, its title, ingredients, and steps are sent to Gemini to fill in what is missing, such as ingredient amounts, and to make the steps easier to follow.
  • Recipe photos you scan are processed by LinkDish's backend and sent to Gemini to turn paper recipes, recipe cards, or cookbook pages into structured recipe details. If Gemini is unavailable, LinkDish can switch photo scans to OpenAI's API from its admin dashboard; your scan images would then be sent to OpenAI for the same purpose only.
  • Personal saved recipes are stored on your device so you can reopen them later inside the app. When you're signed in, your cookbook (saved recipes with your notes, favorites, tags, ratings, collections, and cook log) is also stored by LinkDish's backend with your account so it stays in sync on every device where you sign in. Photos you scan to import a recipe are sent for processing but aren't saved with your cookbook. The copy stays on your device. Free accounts can keep up to 15 personal recipes on a device; paid plans remove that save limit.
  • Shared Family recipes are stored by LinkDish's backend so active household members can view them, duplicate them, and see who added them.
  • The app-generated purchase ID is sent to RevenueCat and to LinkDish's backend service so LinkDish can check entitlement status and apply the correct import and extraction limits.
  • Basic server-side operational logs may be used to monitor reliability, prevent abuse, and debug failures.
  • Analytics are used to understand whether LinkDish is working across the website, web app, Android app, and backend, including extraction success rates, source hostnames, performance problems, and errors.
  • Account and household records are used only to sign you in, manage Family household membership, send login or invite emails through LinkDish's email provider, verify Google/Apple sign-in through LinkDish's configured authentication provider, and apply the shared Family quota.
  • iOS notification list emails are used only to send LinkDish iOS availability updates and maintain the list.

Purchases

LinkDish uses RevenueCat to manage purchase offerings, purchases, restores, customer-portal access, and entitlement checks. Mobile purchases are processed by Apple App Store or Google Play through RevenueCat. Web subscriptions and the one-time Founding offer are processed through RevenueCat Web Billing and its payment processor. LinkDish does not receive your full payment card number or payment account credentials.

Authentication providers

LinkDish may use Clerk to support Google and Apple sign-in. When that sign-in option is enabled, Clerk handles the authentication flow and provides LinkDish with a verified account identifier and email address so LinkDish can connect the sign-in to a LinkDish account. LinkDish keeps its own account ID for saved recipes, Family households, quotas, and account deletion. When you delete your LinkDish account, LinkDish also deletes your user at Clerk. If you signed in with Apple and delete your account in the iPhone app, Apple asks you to confirm, and LinkDish then revokes its Sign in with Apple access; if that can't be done (for example when you delete from the web or Android), LinkDish tells you how to turn it off in your Apple Account settings.

What LinkDish does not do

  • No account is required for the first 3 free recipe imports.
  • Sign in is required to buy or restore a plan and for Family household sharing.
  • LinkDish does not sell your personal information.
  • LinkDish does not show third-party advertising and does not use your information for targeted advertising.
  • LinkDish does not track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • LinkDish analytics do not store full recipe URLs, recipe titles, ingredients, instructions, uploaded recipe images, raw page text, or LLM prompts.

Data storage

Personal recipes you save are stored locally on your device and, when you're signed in, with your account on LinkDish's backend so they sync between your devices. Shared Family recipes and their notes are stored by LinkDish's backend for household access. Extracted recipe data may also pass through LinkDish's backend service in order to produce results and keep the service running reliably. When LinkDish reads a public recipe page, it may keep the result for up to 7 days under the page's address, not linked to you, so the next person who imports the same link gets it faster. Recipe pictures load through LinkDish's image service, which keeps short-lived copies following the picture site's own caching rules. The RevenueCat app user ID is stored on your device and may be stored by LinkDish's backend for quota checks and by RevenueCat for purchase and entitlement management. Mobile session tokens use operating-system-backed secure storage; the web app uses browser storage needed to keep you signed in. Server-side account, session, external sign-in identity, synced cookbook, invite, household, and shared recipe records are stored by LinkDish's backend. iOS notification list records are stored by LinkDish's backend. First-party analytics records are stored by LinkDish's backend using anonymous or hashed identifiers. Android crash reports are processed and stored by Sentry. Website page-view statistics are processed and stored in aggregate by Plausible Analytics.

How long information is kept

  • Your cookbook and account records: until you delete them or your account.
  • Backups: deleted data can stay in encrypted backups for up to 90 days.
  • A stripped record that an account was deleted: 90 days, after which it is deleted automatically. See the account deletion page.
  • Analytics, including anonymous analytics recorded before you signed in: 90 days, after which they are deleted automatically.
  • Crash reports at Sentry: up to 90 days, after which Sentry deletes them.
  • Server logs: up to 90 days.

Who processes your information

LinkDish uses service providers to run the app: hosting and storage (Vercel, Upstash, [TBD: analytics database host]), website hosting and encrypted backups (GitHub), sign-in (Clerk), billing (RevenueCat and its payment processor, and Google Play), email delivery (Resend and Cloudflare), photo scans, video reading, inspired recipes and Fill in with AI (Google's Gemini API; OpenAI's API only if photo scans are switched to it), crash reports (Sentry), and website statistics (Plausible).

Apart from hosting, storage, backups, email delivery and the app stores, the outside services that receive information about you are Clerk, RevenueCat, Google's Gemini API (or OpenAI's API for photo scans, only if switched as described above), Sentry (in the Android app only) and Plausible (on the linkdish.ca website only). LinkDish's analytics are its own first-party analytics. LinkDish does not use any other analytics, advertising or tracking services.

Your choices

You can stop using the extraction service at any time by not submitting links or recipe images, and you can remove personal saved recipes from inside the app whenever you want. You can unshare recipes you added to a Family recipe book from the shared recipe controls.

You can manage or restore access from LinkDish's Plans screen. Store subscriptions are managed through the App Store or Google Play account used to make the purchase; web subscriptions are managed through the RevenueCat customer portal opened from LinkDish. Founding access is a one-time purchase and does not renew.

If you create a LinkDish account, you can delete it from Account in the app. Account deletion removes server-side account, synced cookbook, recipe picture, household, and shared Family recipe records that belong to the account, and the analytics records linked to the account. It also deletes your user at Clerk, our sign-in provider, and your customer record at RevenueCat, our billing provider, and in the iPhone app it revokes Sign in with Apple. Some records are kept, including a stripped record that the account was deleted and the purchase records that the App Store, Google Play, or our payment processor keep themselves. Deletion does not cancel App Store, Google Play, or web subscriptions. Local saved recipes remain on your device. The account deletion page explains what is deleted and what is kept.

If you joined the iOS notification list and want to be removed, or if you cannot access the app and need help with deletion of data retained by LinkDish, email support@linkdish.ca.
