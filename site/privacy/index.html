What LinkDish handles, why it is needed, where it goes, and the choices you have.

LinkDish helps you turn recipe links into a clean, readable recipe format. This page explains what information the app handles and how it is used.

Information LinkDish handles

  • Recipe links that you paste into the app so the service can extract recipe information.
  • Recipe photos or camera captures you choose to submit for image-based recipe scanning.
  • Pictures you choose for your saved recipes. We store a resized copy with its metadata (such as location) removed, at an unlisted public link that anyone holding the link can open without signing in, so your devices and household can show it. We delete them when you delete your account.
  • Recipe content returned from the extraction service, such as titles, ingredients, steps, and timing.
  • Recipes, edits, and personal notes you choose to save. Free includes up to 15 personal saved recipes; paid plans include unlimited personal saves.
  • Recipes, edits, and personal notes you choose to add to a shared Family recipe book, including the account email shown as the recipe owner.
  • Source page text, metadata, video transcripts, and video content needed to extract recipe information from supported links.
  • An app-generated RevenueCat app user ID used to load purchase options, restore purchases, and verify LinkDish paid plan entitlement status.
  • Purchase, subscription, and lifetime-access status returned by RevenueCat, Apple App Store, Google Play, or RevenueCat Web Billing, such as whether a LinkDish Plus, Family, or Founding entitlement is active.
  • If you sign in for plan access or Family household sharing, your account email, login code/session records or social sign-in identity details, household membership, and household invite records.
  • If you choose to set them, a display name of up to 40 characters and an avatar emoji, shown to you and to the other members of your Family household.
  • If you join the iOS notification list, the email address you submit and basic request details needed to prevent abuse.
  • First-party analytics events such as page or screen views, app opens, feature actions, extraction outcomes, source hostnames, app version, session identifiers, and sanitized error summaries.
  • In the Android app, crash and error reports sent to Sentry when the app crashes or stops responding: the error and where in the app code it happened, recent app events, the app version, and the device model and operating system version. LinkDish does not attach your account details to these reports, leaves out console logs, and removes query strings from web addresses in them.
  • On the linkdish.ca website only, page-view statistics collected by Plausible Analytics: the page visited, the referring site, browser, operating system, device type, and country. Plausible does not set cookies and, according to its data policy, does not store IP addresses.

How the information is used

  • Submitted links are sent to LinkDish's backend service to fetch and extract recipe details.
  • Recipe content may be shared with Google Gemini to extract or complete recipe details, as explained in Google Gemini and AI processing.
  • Submitted recipe images may be processed by LinkDish's backend and Google Gemini to turn paper recipes, recipe cards, or cookbook pages into structured recipe details. These scanning requests are separate from pictures you upload to display on saved recipes.
  • Personal saved recipes are stored on your device so you can reopen them later inside the app. When you're signed in, your cookbook (saved recipes with your notes, favorites, tags, ratings, collections, and cook log) is also stored by LinkDish's backend with your account so it stays in sync on every device where you sign in. Image data submitted for scanning is transmitted for processing as described above. Free accounts can keep up to 15 personal recipes on a device; paid plans remove that save limit.
  • Shared Family recipes are stored by LinkDish's backend so active household members can view them, duplicate them, and see who added them.
  • The app-generated purchase ID is sent to RevenueCat and to LinkDish's backend service so LinkDish can check entitlement status and apply the correct import and extraction limits.
  • Basic server-side operational logs may be used to monitor reliability, prevent abuse, and debug failures.
  • Analytics are used to understand whether LinkDish is working across the website, web app, iPhone app, Android app, and backend, including extraction success rates, source hostnames, performance problems, and errors.
  • Account and household records are used only to sign you in, manage Family household membership, send login or invite emails through LinkDish's email provider, verify Google/Apple sign-in through LinkDish's configured authentication provider, and apply the shared Family quota.
  • iOS notification list emails are used only to send LinkDish iOS availability updates and maintain the list.

Google Gemini and AI processing

LinkDish uses Google Gemini, a third-party AI service, to read recipe content and fill in missing recipe details. Content needed for an import or completion request is sent through LinkDish's servers and may be shared with Gemini. This can include pasted text, recipe details, photos you select or capture, and content from submitted links, including video content for supported video imports. Submitting a photo for scanning sends image data off your device; scanning is not an on-device-only operation.

In the iPhone app, LinkDish asks for your permission before sending content for AI processing. You can choose Allow and continue or Don't allow. Your choice is remembered on that device, and you can change it under Account > AI data sharing. Declining cancels the AI request; you can still view saved recipes. Changing your choice applies to future requests and does not undo processing that has already happened.

Only submit content you want processed. AI results can be incomplete or incorrect; check ingredients, quantities and cooking instructions before using them.

Gemini is the only AI provider currently used in production. LinkDish uses the paid Gemini API; Google does not use submitted prompts, files or responses to improve its products under its paid-service terms. Google may retain data for abuse prevention, security and legal obligations as described in those terms; this is not a promise of zero retention.

The consent controls above describe the iPhone app. Android controls depend on the installed version. The web app currently sends content for processing when you use an import or AI-completion feature, without a separate consent prompt.

Purchases

LinkDish uses RevenueCat to manage purchase offerings, purchases, restores, customer-portal access, and entitlement checks. Mobile purchases are processed by Apple App Store or Google Play through RevenueCat. Web subscriptions and the one-time Founding offer are processed through RevenueCat Web Billing and its payment processor. LinkDish does not receive your full payment card number or payment account credentials.

Authentication providers

LinkDish may use Clerk to support Google and Apple sign-in. When that sign-in option is enabled, Clerk handles the authentication flow and provides LinkDish with a verified account identifier and email address so LinkDish can connect the sign-in to a LinkDish account. LinkDish keeps its own account ID for saved recipes, Family households, quotas, and account deletion.

What LinkDish does not do

  • No account is required for the first 3 free recipe imports.
  • Sign in is required to buy or restore a plan and for Family household sharing.
  • LinkDish does not sell your personal information.
  • LinkDish does not include third-party advertising in the current app.
  • LinkDish analytics do not store full recipe URLs, recipe titles, ingredients, instructions, uploaded recipe images, raw page text, or LLM prompts.

Data storage

Personal recipes you save are stored locally on your device and, when you're signed in, with your account on LinkDish's backend so they sync between your devices. Shared Family recipes and their notes are stored by LinkDish's backend for household access. Extracted recipe data may also pass through LinkDish's backend service in order to produce results and keep the service running reliably. The RevenueCat app user ID is stored on your device and may be stored by LinkDish's backend for quota checks and by RevenueCat for purchase and entitlement management. Mobile session tokens use operating-system-backed secure storage; the web app uses browser storage needed to keep you signed in. Server-side account, session, external sign-in identity, synced cookbook, invite, household, and shared recipe records are stored by LinkDish's backend. iOS notification list records are stored by LinkDish's backend. First-party analytics records are stored by LinkDish's backend using anonymous or hashed identifiers. Android crash reports are processed and stored by Sentry. Website page-view statistics are processed and stored in aggregate by Plausible Analytics.

How long information is kept

  • Account and synced cookbook records are kept until you delete them or your account.
  • Deleted data may remain in backups for up to 90 days before rotation.
  • First-party analytics records and Android crash reports at Sentry are kept for up to 90 days.
  • Operational and security logs are retained to run the service, investigate failures and prevent abuse. Administrative audit records are maintained separately for security and accountability.
  • A stripped record of account deletion and sign-in identifiers used to prevent reuse may remain after deletion. The current backend does not automatically expire those records. Contact support to request review or deletion of retained personal data.
  • App stores and service providers may retain their own records under their policies and legal obligations. Deleting your LinkDish account does not cancel a store subscription.

Service providers

LinkDish uses hosting, database, storage, backup and email-delivery providers to operate its backend. First-party analytics are stored in a hosted database on LinkDish's behalf. The app also uses Clerk for authentication, RevenueCat for purchase and entitlement management, and Google Gemini for AI recipe processing. Apple App Store, Google Play and web payment services handle purchases on their respective platforms. Sentry handles crash reports in the Android app only; Plausible collects website statistics on linkdish.ca only. LinkDish does not use this information for cross-app advertising tracking.

Your choices

You can stop using the extraction service at any time by not submitting links, text or recipe images, and you can remove personal saved recipes from inside the app whenever you want. You can unshare recipes you added to a Family recipe book from the shared recipe controls.

You can manage or restore access from LinkDish's Plans screen. Store subscriptions are managed through the App Store or Google Play account used to make the purchase; web subscriptions are managed through the RevenueCat customer portal opened from LinkDish. Founding access is a one-time purchase and does not renew.

If you create a LinkDish account, you can delete it from Account in the app. Account deletion removes server-side account, synced cookbook, household, and shared Family recipe records that belong to the account, and the analytics records linked to the account, but it does not cancel App Store, Google Play, or web subscriptions. Local saved recipes remain on your device. The account deletion page explains what is deleted and what is kept.

If you joined the iOS notification list and want to be removed, or if you cannot access the app and need help with deletion of data retained by LinkDish, email support@linkdish.ca.
